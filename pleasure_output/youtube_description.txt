Picture a night 40,000 years ago: no phone, no screen, just a fire and six long hours of darkness. Long before Netflix, casinos or Spotify, our ancestors had to invent fun from scratch, and what they left behind is surprising: a flute carved from mammoth ivory, dice made from ankle bones, an Egyptian board game for the afterlife, and cave paintings that seem to move in the firelight.

In this episode:
• Why Stone Age life wasn't one endless fight for survival
• How many hours hunter-gatherers really work, and what they did with the rest of the day
• Why boredom may be one of the oldest forces in human history
• The 40,000-year-old mammoth-ivory flute from Germany's Swabian Jura
• How making music together lines up our breathing, bodies and brains
• Knucklebones: nature's dice and the first gambling
• Senet, the Egyptian game buried with Tutankhamun
• Chauvet Cave and the first "movie night"
• How cities turned fun into a business
• Why your brain still craves it every night
