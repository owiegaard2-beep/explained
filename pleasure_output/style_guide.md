# Style guide — What Did Ancient Humans Do For Pleasure?

Same art style as "Why Did Humans Start Wearing Clothes?" (the clothes character sheet is used as the style anchor).

Drawing style: hand-drawn cartoon explainer. Thick, slightly wobbly black marker outlines, flat colors, no gradients,
one soft grey shadow under feet. Stick figures with large round heads, black dot eyes, small curved mouths,
thin black stick limbs with round black hands/feet.
Backgrounds: a simple flat scene (sky + ground, cave wall, desert, city) OR plain off-white paper for symbols/diagrams.
Text cards: flat navy background.

Palette (color = meaning):
- Navy        #3B4A78 — night, darkness, boredom, mystery, text cards
- Fire orange #F08A24 — fire, warmth, fun, pleasure, the "glow" of entertainment
- Hide brown  #8B5A3C — ancestors, fur, earth, cave walls
- Sand ochre  #E2C46A — daylight work, desert, Egypt & Mesopotamia
- Sky blue    #8CCDF2 — daytime, free time, calm
- Red         #E0453A — emphasis, arrows, X marks, danger, risk
(Ice blue only for snow/cold, green tick only for "yes/works". Bone and ivory objects are cream.)

On-image text: bold rounded hand-lettered capitals. White on navy cards, black on light backgrounds. Max 4 words.

Characters:
- The ancestor ("you" in the story): round brown head, short black curly hair, shaggy hide-brown fur wrap over one shoulder.
  Other Ice Age people: same style, varied hair, fur wraps.
- The modern man (same as the clothes video): round white head, messy black hair, orange t-shirt, blue jeans.
- Egyptians: white linen, black bob hair. Mesopotamians: ochre robes, beards.

Recurring symbols (always drawn like their reference): campfire, bone flute, knucklebones, Senet board, brain character.
