# Style guide — Why Did Humans Start Wearing Clothes?

Drawing style: hand-drawn cartoon explainer. Thick, slightly wobbly black marker outlines, flat colors, no gradients,
one soft grey shadow under feet. Stick figures with large round heads, black dot eyes, small curved mouths,
thin black stick limbs with round black hands/feet, bean-shaped colored torso = clothing (or bare skin).
Backgrounds: a simple flat scene (sky + ground, a few plants) OR plain off-white paper for symbols/diagrams.
Text cards: flat navy background.

Palette (color = meaning):
- Sky blue  #8CCDF2 — daytime, outdoors, calm
- Sand ochre #E2C46A — heat, sun, savanna, dry scrub
- Hide brown #8B5A3C — fur, hides, leather, ancestors' skin
- Navy      #3B4A78 — text cards, night, mystery, questions
- Red       #E0453A — emphasis, arrows, X marks, danger, shame/blushing
- Ice blue  #DDEFF7 — cold, snow, ice age
(Green tick only for "yes/works".)

On-image text: bold rounded hand-lettered capitals. White on navy cards, black on light backgrounds. Max 4 words.

Main character: modern young man — big round white head, short messy black hair, orange t-shirt, blue jeans.
Ancestors: same style, round brown heads, short black curly hair, brown bare-skin torso, walking stick.
Neanderthals: same style, stockier body, heavier brow.

Recurring symbols (always drawn like their reference): animal hide pelt, body louse, bone needle.
