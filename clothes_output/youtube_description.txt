Every other animal gets by with fur, feathers or scales. We are the only ones who get dressed. So when did it start, and why? The first clothes rotted away thousands of years ago, so scientists have had to work it out from what was left behind: a tiny parasite, a gene for dark skin, a pile of bone tools and the eye of a needle.

In this episode:
• Why the first clothes will never be found
• How lice DNA works like a clock for the first clothing
• Why losing our fur came long before getting dressed
• Sweat, bare skin, and the cold nights that changed everything
• "Borrowed fur": why clothes beat growing a thicker coat
• The bone tools and fox skins of Contrebandiers Cave, Morocco
• Bone needles, tailored clothing and the Neanderthal question
• What Ötzi the Iceman was wearing 5,300 years ago
• How clothes became a language, and where the shame of nakedness comes from
