On May 14, 1948, David Ben-Gurion read out a declaration in a small museum in Tel Aviv. The ceremony took about half an hour. The history behind it goes back more than 3,000 years.

This is Part 1 of the history of Israel. It's the story of a narrow corridor between Egypt and Mesopotamia, a city sacred to three faiths, and a people who carried their history far beyond the land where it began.

In this episode:
• Canaan and the land bridge between empires
• The first mention of "Israel" outside the Bible: the Merneptah Stele (c. 1208 BCE)
• The separate kingdoms of Israel and Judah
• The Assyrian conquest (722 BCE) and the Babylonian destruction of the First Temple (586 BCE)
• Exile, the Persian return under Cyrus and the Second Temple
• Alexander the Great, the Seleucids, the Maccabean Revolt and the Hasmonean kingdom
• Roman Judea, the Jewish-Roman War and the destruction of the Second Temple (70 CE)
• What the Western Wall actually is
• How rabbinic Judaism kept Jewish life going without the Temple
• The Bar Kokhba revolt and how the diaspora grew over centuries
• Constantine, the Church of the Holy Sepulchre (335) and Byzantine Jerusalem
• The 7th-century Islamic conquest and the Dome of the Rock (691/692)
• Why one city holds three sacred histories
• Next episode: the Crusades

Assyria could conquer the north. Babylon could burn the First Temple. Rome could destroy the Second. None of them could erase what Jerusalem meant to the people who remembered it.
